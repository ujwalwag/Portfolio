The thread

Robots that help things grow.

Almost everything I have built points the same direction. A rover that reads soil chemistry and radios it home. A vision model that finds disease on a leaf before a person would. A CNC gantry that plows, seeds and waters a tray without being told twice. A tidying robot that picks up after a household.

None of that is science fiction. It is sensors, a navigation stack, and a lot of patience with the gap between a clean simulation and a muddy field. The interesting problems live in that gap.

I like autonomy pointed at something that is alive — soil, crops, a campus full of people — because those systems refuse to behave, and that is where the engineering is.

I came to robotics sideways: an electronics and instrumentation degree, a stint as a software developer across VR, front-end and infrastructure, then a master's in robotics at SUNY Buffalo and a research post building simulation-first rovers. These days I keep an autonomous fleet running across a campus, which is the least romantic and most instructive robotics job I have had.
